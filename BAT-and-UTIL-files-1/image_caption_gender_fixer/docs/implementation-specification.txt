    image.jpg.bak.202610231430.replaced-by-chatgpt.bak

9. DETERMINISTIC SUBSTITUTION ENGINE
===============================================================================

Support built-in rules, folder-specific learned rules, optional user-global
rules, explicit numeric priorities, Unicode-aware case handling, word-aware
literal matches, punctuation-aware phrases, explicitly enabled regex rules,
and ignore/false-positive rules.

Seed:

    priority=100    boi       -> boy
    priority=10     good boy  -> good girl
    priority=10     boy       -> girl
    priority=10     man       -> woman
    priority=10     husband   -> wife

Required final results:

    boi       -> girl
    good boi  -> good girl
    good boy  -> good girl
    man       -> woman
    husband   -> wife

Priority 100 is a normalization stage. Its output may match a lower-priority
rule. Resolve the entire pipeline before changing pixels and render only the
final result.

For example:

    Original OCR:     good boi
    Priority 100:     boi -> boy
    Intermediate:     good boy
    Priority 10:      good boy -> good girl
    Final rendering:  good girl

ORDER AND OVERLAPS:

1. Process priority levels in descending numeric order.
2. At each priority, collect matches from a snapshot of the text entering
   that stage. Do not rerun rules against text generated within that stage.
3. Resolve overlapping candidates by longest normalized matched source span.
   Use normalized literal phrase length for literals and actual matched text
   length for regex, not the number of characters in regex syntax.
4. At equal priority and match length, prefer in this order:
   - literal over regex;
   - folder-specific over global/default;
   - explicit user rule over built-in;
   - stable creation order.
5. Resolve any remaining overlap tie by the leftmost match.
6. Apply the selected nonoverlapping changes as one stage.

Generated text may be processed only at lower priorities. Process each
priority level once. Set and document a maximum stage count.

Reject direct and indirect literal-rule cycles and invalid rule definitions.
For explicitly enabled regex, validate the supported syntax, use bounded
evaluation, reject detected repeated states/cycles, and prevent pathological
regex execution from hanging a run. Do not claim complete static analysis of
arbitrary regex programs. Restrict unsupported regex behavior rather than
weakening the deterministic or bounded-processing guarantees.

Maintain provenance from transformed characters/phrases back to original OCR
word boxes, even when a normalization changes string length, whitespace
grouping, or case-folding length. Phrase replacement must target the correct
original pixels, not guessed intermediate coordinates.

MATCH SAFETY:

- Match whole words or phrases by default.
- Do not replace substrings inside unrelated words.
- man must not match inside woman.
- Substring or regex matching needs explicit configuration.
- Preserve surrounding punctuation and whitespace unless a rule changes them.
- Transfer ordinary case sensibly: BOY -> GIRL, Boy -> Girl, boy -> girl.
- Mixed or unusual capitalization requires review or an explicit policy.
- Never alter low-confidence OCR merely because it resembles a known rule.

Maintain an explainable trace of rule IDs, priorities, spans, intermediate
transformations, and final text. Keep the necessary trace local in transaction
state; do not put source/replacement text in image markers or processed history.

workflow.

12. THREE VISUAL-CONFIDENCE LEVELS
===============================================================================

Classify each proposed image edit:

LEVEL 1 — CONFIDENT:

- high OCR confidence;
- precise text mask;
- flat or reconstructable background;
- confidently estimated text styling;
- replacement fits safely;
- verification succeeds.

LEVEL 2 — REVIEW REQUIRED:

- mild texture or gradient;
- approximate font match;
- modest resizing or repositioning;
- uncertain outline, shadow, spacing, or punctuation;
- potentially visible but manageable repair seam.

LEVEL 3 — DIFFICULT:

- text over a photograph or detailed artwork;
- text crossing a face, hand, object, or strong edge;
- curved, rotated, perspective-distorted, or highly styled text;
- complex outline, glow, transparency, or shadow;
- uncertain background reconstruction;
- inadequate safe space.

These levels control warnings and tools. They do not normally bypass review.

===============================================================================
13. MANDATORY PER-IMAGE APPROVAL AND CANCELLATION
===============================================================================

Every image with a proposed change requires individual approval before commit,
including Level 1 images by default.

For an image containing multiple substitutions:

1. Permit per-region adjustments.
2. Assemble one composite final proposal.
3. Show the complete proposal when previews are enabled.
4. Request one final approval for that complete image.

Only this explicit option may bypass review for an otherwise safe Level 1 edit:

    --skip-reivew-for-confident-ones

Support that exact spelling and the identical correctly spelled alias:

    --skip-review-for-confident-ones

Neither option may bypass Level 2 or Level 3 review, a hard-link warning,
source-integrity failure, ambiguous history, unsafe placement, or other
filesystem decisions requiring explicit approval.

Without one of those aliases, --yes, remembered substitutions, a previous
approval, and batch operation must not bypass per-image approval.

Explain that visual editing remains beta-quality and deliberate individual
approval is the default. File safety and recovery must still be engineered
and tested to the acceptance standard.

Provide review choices such as:

    Approve and continue
    Approve and pause
    Skip and continue
    Skip and pause
    Defer until the end
    Fix detected text / OCR
    Adjust visual rendering
    Compare background repairs
    Cancel the current uncommitted edit

Approval applies to the actual final proposal and validated source identity.
Changing text, masks, styling, repair, placement, output pixels, or relevant
metadata invalidates the previous approval and requires a new final approval,
unless a freshly verified Level 1 proposal qualifies for the explicit bypass.

SKIPPING AND CANCELLATION MEAN:

- The source image bytes, metadata, and filename remain unchanged.
- No corrected output is committed.
- No successful conversion is written to .history.icgf.
- No learned rule or visual preference is silently saved.
- A rule the user separately requested saving may remain.
- The session may remember skipped, deferred, failed, or canceled outcomes.
- Necessary recovery bookkeeping may persist.
- Program-owned candidate renders may be safely cleaned up.

Remembering "this picture was skipped" is permitted and is needed for useful
resume behavior. It does not mean that image was successfully processed.

With --no-preview, retain the approval step and clearly summarize the changes.
In JSON or other noninteractive operation, never prompt; return user-selection-
required for an edit needing approval. Only an explicitly requested confident-
review bypass can permit an otherwise fully verified Level 1 automatic commit.

===============================================================================
14. TEXT MASKS AND PHOTOGRAPHIC BACKGROUND REPAIR
===============================================================================

Do not erase an entire OCR rectangle when a more precise glyph mask can be
built.

Construct the smallest practical mask covering:

- foreground glyphs;
- antialiased edges;
- outlines;
- shadows;
- glows;
- other detected text effects.

Dilate the mask only enough to remove visible remnants.

Preserve every unaffected background pixel exactly where practical.

Provide text-driven mask adjustments:

    expand mask by one pixel
    contract mask by one pixel
    include or exclude shadow
    include or exclude outline
    extend left, right, up, or down
    protect a neighboring region
    reset mask
    compare repair candidates

Show:

- original crop;
- mask overlay;
- repair-only crop;
- final crop.

Do not replace a photographic region with a flat rectangle unless the user
explicitly chooses a solid caption-label treatment.

Use progressively capable local repair strategies:

1. Solid or nearly solid fill sampled from several surrounding regions.
2. Directional interpolation for simple gradients.
3. Classical local inpainting for textured or photographic backgrounds.
4. Patch-based reconstruction using nearby visual material.
5. Optional heavier local inpainting only with explicit approval.

OpenCV native inpainting through `opencv-python-headless` and NumPy is
permitted because photographic backgrounds are an explicit requirement.

If multiple lightweight repairs are plausible, render candidates and allow the
user to choose.

Do not install or use a large neural inpainting model automatically.

Before offering one, explain:

- download size;
- disk usage;
- CPU or GPU use;
- electricity implications;
- license;
- network requirements;
- local privacy behavior.

15. SOURCE-TEXT STYLE IMITATION
===============================================================================

Attempt to reproduce:

- likely font family;
- font weight;
- italic or slant;
- font size;
- capitalization;
- letter spacing;
- word spacing;
- baseline;
- alignment;
- foreground color;
- opacity;
- outline color and width;
- shadow color;
- shadow offset;
- shadow blur;
- simple glow or effects.

Tesseract does not reliably identify exact fonts. Do not claim an exact match
unless independently established.

Rank plausible installed fonts by comparing rendered source characters against
the extracted source mask.

Do not install a new font automatically.

For uncertain style matching, show labeled candidates.

Allow adjustments including:

    smaller or larger
    move left, right, up, or down
    narrower or wider tracking
    choose another font
    change foreground color
    change outline
    change shadow
    choose background repair
    show mask
    render again
    skip region
    cancel image

Remember a visual-style preference only when explicitly requested.

Learning `boy -> girl` must not automatically authorize a particular font,
color, placement, mask, or background-repair method.

===============================================================================
16. REPLACEMENT FITTING AND COLLISION SAFETY
===============================================================================

Before rendering:

1. OCR and protect neighboring text regions.
2. Determine image boundaries and safe placement.
3. Measure the fully styled replacement, including:
   - outline;
   - shadow;
   - glow;
   - antialiasing margin.
4. Verify it will not:
   - overlap another protected word;
   - obscure neighboring punctuation;
   - unnecessarily cover an important image feature;
   - extend beyond the image;
   - be clipped.

Attempt fitting in this order:

1. Preserve original font size, baseline, and alignment.
2. Expand into verified-safe adjacent background.
3. Shift slightly into safe space while preserving reading order.
4. Reduce letter spacing conservatively.
5. Reduce font size by no more than approximately 15–20%.
6. Require manual review and adjustment.

Never silently:

- overlap another word;
- go outside the image;
- clip the replacement;
- cover neighboring punctuation;
- stretch or squash letters;
- wrap a single word;
- make text unreadably small.

If no safe placement exists, classify the edit as Level 3 and offer:

- manual adjustment;
- alternative wording;
- solid caption label;
- skip;
- cancel.

===============================================================================
17. IMAGE AND PHOTOGRAPHIC VALIDATION
===============================================================================

Preserve where safely possible:

- dimensions;
- orientation;
- alpha;
- ICC profile;
- relevant EXIF;
- frame count;
- animation;
- image quality.

Avoid unnecessary recompression.

Do not silently edit only the first frame of an animated or multiframe image.
Preserve the complete image or explicitly defer it.

For photographic repairs:

- compare repaired boundaries with nearby gradients and edges;
- calculate a seam or discontinuity warning score;
- detect obvious repetition or smearing where practical;
- protect faces and important objects where detectable;
- classify uncertain repairs as Level 3;
- require review.

Before final approval:

- verify decoding;
- verify dimensions;
- verify orientation;
- verify frame count;
- verify metadata handling;
- verify that rendered pixels remain inside the image;
- verify that protected neighboring text boxes were not crossed;
- rerun targeted OCR on corrected regions;
- confirm the replacement where feasible;
- confirm the source phrase is absent where feasible;
- display every uncertain or failed check.

Aesthetic quality cannot be proven numerically. Human approval remains required
unless the confident-review bypass option was explicitly supplied and every
Level 1 requirement is satisfied.

If all candidate regions are rejected or skipped:

- do not rename the source;
- do not create a corrected output;
- do not record a successful conversion.

For lossless output, test that decoded pixels outside the affected repair/
render region remain identical where no global transformation is required.
JPEG re-encoding can change pixels outside that region; document this limitation
and avoid claiming bit-identical unaffected pixels for lossy re-encoding.
The preserved original backup must always remain byte-for-byte identical.

Do not both bake an orientation transform into pixels and retain an EXIF value
that would rotate the output again. Validate displayed orientation as well as
stored dimensions. Preserve metadata deliberately rather than copying stale
dimension/orientation tags blindly.

===============================================================================
18. OUTPUT AND ORIGINAL-BACKUP TRANSACTION
===============================================================================

A successful conversion of image.jpg produces these sibling files:

    image.jpg.bak.YYYYMMDDHHMM.replaced-by-chatgpt.bak
    image-gendeerfixed.jpg

The original filename is absent after successful replacement because the
unchanged original has been renamed to its verified backup. The corrected
image uses the corrected suffix. Do not invent another backup convention.

Use a recoverable multi-step transaction, not an unsupported claim that
multiple separate file operations and history appends are one atomic action.

1. Inspect the source, identify its format and file identity, and hash it.
2. Consult folder history and product-detection checks before expensive OCR.
3. Resolve OCR and the complete final substitution pipeline.
4. Create uniquely named candidate/staging images directly beside the source.
5. Validate decoding, dimensions, displayed orientation, frames, metadata,
   protected regions, text fit, and other required checks.
6. Obtain the required approval for the actual final proposal.
7. Acquire appropriate source and folder-history locks. Use Windows sharing/
   locking behavior that prevents source mutation through the final validation
   and rename window where supported. If this cannot be made safe, stop.
8. Revalidate source identity and rehash it immediately before commit. Abort
   if it changed. Never treat a stale earlier hash as sufficient.
9. Reserve collision-safe backup and corrected-output names without overwrites.
10. Durably write a transaction intent/recovery journal before the first rename.
11. Rename the original to its required sibling backup without overwriting.
12. Verify the preserved original's identity/hash and journal the transition.
13. Rename the approved, verified staging output to the corrected filename,
    then verify its identity/hash and journal the transition.
14. Append and flush the authoritative committed event to the source folder's
    .history.icgf last. Use an idempotent transaction/event identity.
15. Mark the local transaction and session committed. Update any derived
    cross-folder cache only after the authoritative history commit.

Never declare success before both the image transaction and the authoritative
history event are durably committed. Failure to update a derived cache alone
must not erase or invalidate a committed folder-history transaction.

If an image commit step or the history commit fails, roll back safely where
possible. Never overwrite a newly occupied path during rollback. If rollback
cannot be completed, retain all recoverable originals and artifacts, journal
the exact state, and report a recoverable partial failure.

A crash after a valid history append but before a local "committed" marker
must be recognized as the same already committed transaction on recovery.
Do not duplicate it or undo it merely because local state lagged.

Never overwrite an existing output or backup. Example output collisions:

    image-gendeerfixed (1).jpg
    image-gendeerfixed (2).jpg

Use per-target locks and a folder-history write lock so concurrent cooperating
instances cannot process the same file or corrupt .history.icgf.
Recover stale locks conservatively and verify every rename or move.

===============================================================================
19. PREVIOUSLY PROCESSED IMAGES AND PRODUCT DETECTION
===============================================================================

Strongly avoid processing the same image or a program product twice.

Use multiple layers:

1. Recognize the -gendeerfixed suffix, including collision-numbered names.
2. Recognize original replacement-backup names.
3. Recognize corrected undo-archive names.
4. Exclude known preview, crop, mask, and staging artifacts.
5. Calculate original and corrected SHA-256 hashes.
6. Consult active and undone transactions in the image folder's .history.icgf.
7. Read a non-sensitive embedded application marker where the format supports it.
8. Maintain a derived cross-folder hash cache from histories already observed.
9. Optionally calculate perceptual hashes and normalized OCR fingerprints.

An image marker may contain the application identifier, marker schema,
transaction UUID, timestamp, original SHA-256, and rules fingerprint.
Do not embed filenames, paths, OCR text, or replacement/substitution text.
Hash the actual final output, including its final marker/metadata, for history.

Skip an exact known corrected-output hash even when its filename changes.
Skip or report an exact original hash belonging to an active conversion.
Recognize hashes and transaction identities rather than relying on filenames
alone. A misleading filename or conflicting marker/history must not justify
silently skipping an ambiguous unrelated image.

Perceptual similarity alone must not silently skip an image. Present probable
re-encoded duplicates for review. If a filename, marker, and history disagree,
report the ambiguity and require a safe explicit decision.

After undo, identify the original as previously processed and undone. Permit
deliberate reprocessing with explicit review rather than treating it as an
already active conversion or silently converting it again.

The per-folder .history.icgf is authoritative. The cross-folder cache under
runtime\cache is only a derived accelerator; folder history must work with an
empty, missing, stale, or unavailable cache.

Renaming or moving the containing image folder together with its history must
not defeat processed-image recognition. Use relative filenames, hashes, and
transaction IDs, not absolute folder names or drive letters, as the basis.

Document the limits honestly: folder-contained known hashes and markers can
recognize copies and renames reliably within available history. Moving an
image alone without its history or marker, using a different installation
with no relevant history, arbitrary crops, screenshots, re-encoding, and heavy
editing may evade detection. Do not claim a machine-global guarantee without
available evidence.

===============================================================================
20. HIDDEN PER-FOLDER HISTORY: .history.icgf
===============================================================================

Store authoritative processing history in the actual folder containing each
processed source image, under exactly:

    .history.icgf

For a recursive run, each image-containing directory records its own committed
image transactions. Do not place the only history in the tool's installation
or in one unrelated parent folder.

Set the Windows Hidden attribute, preserving other appropriate attributes.
A leading dot in the filename is not a substitute for explicitly setting
that attribute. If the required attribute or safe history writes cannot be
established, report the issue and do not silently claim full success.

Use human-readable UTF-8, versioned, append-oriented JSON Lines or an equally
transparent documented record format. Do not use DPAPI or any encryption.
Sessions, journals, preferences, rules, and derived caches are also unencrypted.

Include a recognizable application/schema header and stable history identity.
Validate an existing file before writing. Preserve unrelated or unsupported
files; do not overwrite them or create a numbered "fresh history" to ignore
their contents.

Transaction/event records should contain:

- schema version;
- unique event ID and cryptographically random transaction UUID;
- event type and ordered sequence information;
- timezone-aware timestamp;
- original, corrected, original-backup, and undo-archive names as applicable,
  stored relative to this image folder;
- original and corrected SHA-256 hashes;
- sizes and useful modification times;
- rules/profile fingerprint and program version;
- active, undone, recovered, or other documented transaction state;
- enough integrity/recovery information to distinguish committed operations.

Use relative paths only for records referring to files in this folder.
Do not bind folder history to an absolute parent pathname, drive letter,
computer, Windows user account, or machine-bound encryption key.

Reject unsafe path components, absolute paths, traversal outside the recorded
folder, and reparse-point escapes when resolving history references. Treat
history as data to validate, not executable instructions or trusted arbitrary
filesystem destinations.

Do not put OCR text, replacement text, or full rule traces in processed-image
history. Keep rules in the separate .image-gender-fixer.stt sidecar. A needed
local rule trace may live in the transaction journal.

Use locking and durable writes. Detect malformed/corrupted records, partial
final writes, inconsistent sequence/order, duplicate event IDs, and
inconsistent state transitions. Sequence information and a record-hash chain
may detect accidental damage; do not describe unencrypted records as providing
authenticated protection against a person deliberately rewriting the file.

Retain readable history when a final record is incomplete. Do not append new
records into a malformed tail or silently discard damaged bytes. Report the
issue and use an explicit safe recovery path before further mutations.
Recovery must preserve enough evidence to resolve interrupted transactions.

Create .history.icgf only when meaningful transaction/recovery state actually
needs recording. A canceled/skipped proposal does not require a successful
conversion record. No-match scans and read-only commands must not create it.

Successful image commits and undos are appended as events; do not erase the
earlier conversion to record an undo. A transaction's latest validated state
controls whether its original is eligible for deliberate reprocessing.

History must survive a rename or move of the containing folder together with
its contents. Existing local session portability and cross-computer undo are
not required, but plain folder history must remain readable and useful for
processed-image detection on another installation.

Implement safe history status, verification, and explicitly requested backup
commands. An explicit history backup must not overwrite anything and must
not become a second competing authoritative history.

Use restrictive Windows permissions where practical without binding records
to a particular account. Do not add a separate .processed.enc registry or
require a central index for normal folder-based recognition.

===============================================================================
21. AUTOMATIC OLD-JOB DISCOVERY, PAUSE, AND RESUME
===============================================================================

Large review runs must be safely resumable on the current installation.
Store unencrypted session journals under:

    runtime\state\sessions\

Record completed/committed, skipped, deferred, failed, not-yet-reviewed,
currently interrupted, and uncommitted states. Identify items by hashes,
transaction identity, and validated file references, not queue positions alone.

Never mark an image complete until its image transaction and authoritative
.history.icgf event commit. Persisting a skipped/canceled session outcome is
permitted; it must not create a successful image-history event.

When an ordinary processing command starts, before creating a new job:

1. Search automatically for resumable old jobs.
2. Display each job's session ID, target description, creation/last-activity
   times, completed/remaining/failed counts, and any interrupted transaction.
3. Offer:
   - resume a selected old job;
   - inspect details;
   - trash and forget it;
   - leave it untouched and start a new job;
   - cancel.
4. Support selecting and handling multiple existing jobs individually.

"Trash and forget" means:

- recycle the session's uncommitted queue, session-owned state, and safely
  disposable owned artifacts;
- account for adjacent previews/staging images using the exact ownership
  manifest rather than arbitrary filename patterns;
- remove the job from future resume prompts only after successful abandonment;
- preserve committed corrected images and original backups;
- preserve .history.icgf and committed undo/conversion events;
- preserve learned rules and user preferences;
- explain that forgetting a job does not undo completed image changes.

Do not abandon a job containing an interrupted filesystem transaction until
recovery or rollback establishes a safe state. Explain the blocking state.
Never discard an ambiguous image or its recovery evidence.

If recycling abandonment data fails, retain the session and its recovery
information, report the failure, and do not falsely mark it forgotten.

Support:

    --resume
    --resume <session-id>
    --new-session
    sessions list
    sessions status <session-id>
    sessions abandon <session-id>

--resume with one eligible job may select it; with multiple jobs it must ask
for a selection, or report selection-required noninteractively. An explicit
session ID must resolve uniquely.

--new-session leaves old jobs untouched and reports that they remain.
Revalidate sources, current rules, and already committed history when resuming;
do not reuse a stale proposal's approval after material changes.

Help, version, doctor, read-only rule/history/environment commands, and other
administrative inspections must not force a resume prompt. A dry run may
report old jobs without creating, advancing, or abandoning one.

In JSON/noninteractive processing mode, do not prompt. Report existing jobs
and require an explicit resume/new-session/abandon choice when necessary.
--yes must not silently choose one.

Ctrl+C or terminal closure must preserve the current session and leave the
current image unchanged or recoverable. Do not promise recovery from physical
storage failure; test interrupted operations and use durable journal writes.

Moving live sessions between computers is not required. Folder-based
processed-image recognition must not depend on those sessions being present.

===============================================================================
22. ADJACENT RENDERS AND OWNED TEMPORARY ARTIFACTS
===============================================================================

Write proposed image renders, repaired crops, mask overlays, previews, and the
final staging image directly beside the source image. Do not place these
image files under runtime\temp or another drive.

Use collision-safe, recognizable names containing a random transaction UUID,
for example:

    .icgf-<uuid>-mask.png
    .icgf-<uuid>-repair.png
    .icgf-<uuid>-proposal.jpg

Set Hidden attributes where appropriate. Naming alone is not sufficient proof
of ownership: record exact artifact paths, identities, and transaction/session
association. Never claim, replace, or recycle a pre-existing similarly named
file merely because it matches a prefix.

Minimize files by retaining small intermediates in memory where practical.
Keep nonimage logs, locks, state, and caches in their designated tool
subdirectories. Create no adjacent artifacts during dry runs or no-match scans.

A candidate image is disposable only when the transaction no longer needs it.
After commit, rejection, cancellation, or explicit safe session abandonment,
recycle the exact known disposable image artifacts. Verify recycling; if it
fails, retain the files and report their exact locations. Preserve anything
needed for interrupted-transaction recovery.

A file renamed into the final corrected output is no longer temporary.
An original backup is never a disposable preview.
Never delete or recycle an unknown file through wildcard cleanup.

Installers and native tools may manage their own internal temporary files
normally. That permission does not extend to the source, original backups,
corrected undo archives, or ambiguous recovery artifacts.

Doctor may identify abandoned owned previews and staging images and offer
recycling, but must not recycle them without explicit confirmation. Ordinary
cleanup of a current run's known disposable previews does not require repeated
per-preview prompts.

===============================================================================
23. UNDO
===============================================================================

Support:

    image_caption_gender_fixer.py undo *
    image_caption_gender_fixer.py undo whatever.jpg

A target may refer to:

- original name;
- corrected name;
- original backup;
- known transaction;

provided resolution is unambiguous.

Undo restores the original filename whenever safely possible.

The corrected image must not initially be discarded. Rename it to:

    <corrected-filename>.bak.YYYYMMDDHHMM.undone-by-image-caption-gender-fixer.bak

Example before:

    image.jpg.bak.202610221430.replaced-by-chatgpt.bak
    image-gendeerfixed.jpg

Example after:

    image.jpg
    image-gendeerfixed.jpg.bak.202610221455.undone-by-image-caption-gender-fixer.bak

Apply collision numbering before the final `.bak`.

Undo order:

1. Resolve one unambiguous active transaction.
2. Verify the original backup hash.
3. Verify and classify the corrected file.
4. Check whether the original path is occupied.
5. Calculate the undo-archive name.
6. Rename the corrected image to the undo archive.
7. Rename the original backup to the original filename.
8. Verify the restored original byte-for-byte.
9. Roll back the corrected rename if restoration fails where safely possible.
10. Durably append an idempotent undo event to the folder's .history.icgf,
    using a journal so an interrupted restore/history update is recoverable.
11. Apply the undo-archive disposition preference.

Never overwrite a file occupying the original path.

Refuse by default or offer an explicitly confirmed collision-safe alternate.

If the corrected image has changed since creation:

- always retain its undo archive initially;
- never automatically recycle it;
- require explicit per-file confirmation for any later recycling.

If the folder's .history.icgf is missing, unreadable, incompatible, or
ambiguous, normal undo must not guess.

Provide explicit recovery through:

    --recover-from-backups

Show candidates and require confirmation.

Undo must not remove learned rules.

Undo lookup must use validated folder-relative history references, hashes, and
transaction identity. Merely renaming or moving the containing folder must not
invalidate those references. Cross-computer session migration and guaranteed
cross-computer undo are not acceptance requirements.

Journal intent before undo renames and verify the filesystem state after each
transition. A successful undo consumes the active original-backup filename by
renaming that preserved original back to its source filename; this is not a
deletion of original content.

Append the undo state only after the original is safely restored and verified.
Keep conversion history rather than erasing it. If restoration succeeded but
history update failed, retain recovery evidence and report a recoverable
partial failure rather than inventing a clean state.

The initial undo-archive rename preserves the corrected image without changing
its bytes. A changed corrected image always requires its own explicit approval
before any later recycling, regardless of --yes, remembered preferences, or
a run-level "recycle all" answer.

When undo resolves a transaction once through several wildcard/name arguments,
perform it once. Never restore a different image merely because a filename
resembles an expected backup.

===============================================================================
24. POST-UNDO KEEP OR RECYCLE PREFERENCE
===============================================================================

After safely creating the corrected undo archive, offer:

1. Keep this undo archive.
2. Recycle this undo archive.
3. Keep all remaining undo archives this run.
4. Recycle all remaining undo archives this run.
5. Always keep future undo archives.
6. Always recycle future undo archives.
7. Cancel and keep it.

Default to keep.

Support persistent unencrypted preferences:

    ask
    keep
    recycle

Commands:

    preferences show
    preferences set undo-corrected ask
    preferences set undo-corrected keep
    preferences set undo-corrected recycle
    preferences reset undo-corrected

Per-run overrides:

    --ask-about-undo-archives
    --keep-undo-archives
    --recycle-undo-archives

A run-level “all remaining” answer applies only to that invocation.

An “always” answer persists.

Never apply automatic recycling to a corrected archive whose hash differs from
the recorded corrected hash.

If recycling fails, retain and report the archive.

Never permanently delete it.

Store preferences under the tool's runtime state directory, not in the image
folder's history. Use a schema and safe state writes, without image-style
backups on each update. Conflicting per-run archive-disposition flags are
invalid arguments.

In noninteractive/JSON mode, an unspecified ask preference must retain the
archive safely and report that it was kept; it must not prompt or guess
permission to recycle. An explicitly selected keep/recycle policy may be
applied only where all changed-file protections permit it.

===============================================================================
25. FILESYSTEM AND CAPACITY CHECKS
===============================================================================

Before rendering or committing, check realistic available capacity on the
source volume and on any volume holding required transaction state.

Account for candidate/staging outputs, any actually required backup copies,
history and journal growth, and rollback needs. A same-volume rename of an
original into its backup normally does not need a second full image copy;
do not invent unnecessary duplicate-storage requirements.

Detect and handle:

- read-only sources or folders;
- access denial and unavailable history writes;
- path-length limitations;
- files in use;
- unsupported or unreliable filesystem behavior;
- network-drive semantics;
- hard links and reparse points;
- source changes;
- collisions created by another process;
- inability to retain a recoverable original.

Use Windows extended-length-path-safe APIs where practical. Preserve legal
Unicode and unusual filenames through argument arrays and explicit path APIs.

All rendered/staging image files must be beside the source, keeping final
rename operations on the same volume. Do not stage under the tool directory
and then silently fall back to a cross-volume copy/delete.

If the required atomic, non-overwriting operation is unavailable, explain the
limitation and require an explicit safe decision, or defer the image. Never
permanently delete an original or backup as a fallback.

--allow-network only permits considering a remote target. Do not claim remote
atomicity, locking, durability, or recycling is reliable without checking.
Retain files and report when safe processing or recycling cannot be supported.

After each move or rename, verify expected source/destination existence and
the expected identity/hash. Detect an original-name collision during undo
without overwriting it.

Preserve useful timestamps and attributes where safe. Document timestamps or
attributes that necessarily change because a new image was created or a file
was renamed.

===============================================================================
26. CRASH RECOVERY, LOGGING, AND LOCKING
===============================================================================

Maintain unencrypted crash-safe transaction journals under:

    runtime\state\transactions\

Flush intent before protected filesystem mutations and record verified state
transitions. Use the authoritative folder history to reconcile a journal that
lags a committed transaction.

For mutating processing/recovery commands, detect interrupted transactions and:

- complete deterministic recovery;
- roll back safely; or
- stop and explain the specific decision required.

Read-only commands, help/version, explain, and --dry-run may report pending
recovery but must not perform mutations as a startup side effect.

Recovery must be idempotent. Never duplicate a committed history event,
overwrite an unrelated occupant, or classify an ambiguous source/backup/
corrected file by timestamp alone. Preserve all original data and recovery
evidence if the state cannot be resolved safely.

A renamed folder may make a local session's old absolute target hint stale.
Report that situation and use validated selected-folder history, hashes, and
explicit user selection where needed; do not search arbitrary disks or guess
a replacement target. Cross-computer live-session portability is not required.

31. ACCEPTANCE STANDARD
===============================================================================

A successful edit requires:

- a verified byte-for-byte original backup under the required sibling name;
- a decodable corrected output with the -gendeerfixed suffix;
- expected dimensions, displayed orientation, alpha/frames where supported,
  and deliberate metadata handling;
- no unresolved filename collision or silent overwrite;
- no source change between approved analysis and protected commit;
- no known overlap with protected neighboring text or out-of-bounds rendering;
- completed required review of the actual final proposal;
- consistent journal/filesystem state;
- a durable committed event in the correct folder's hidden .history.icgf;
- no new unexpected artifacts in the tool's own project root.

"Processed successfully" means the corrected image and authoritative history
event were actually committed. Skipped, rejected, deferred, canceled, failed,
and merely rendered images are not successful conversions.

Photographic reconstruction and font imitation are best-effort visual tasks.
Implement the required local repair strategies and adjustment/review workflow,
label uncertainty honestly, and permit safe explicit deferral when an image
cannot be edited acceptably. Deferral is not successful conversion.

Unsupported animation/multiframe cases may be explicitly deferred rather than
silently losing frames. Ordinary PNG/JPEG editing, required safe transactions,
folder history, review, sessions, and undo may not be replaced with stubs or
blanket "unsupported" results.

A useful dependency bundle must actually be present and validated. A lockfile,
download offer, README installation instructions, or tests using globally
installed packages alone do not establish bundled-dependency acceptance.
